AI suppliers won, and most of them flagged themselves with one huge-volume day

The biggest US winners of the last five years were mostly not the famous AI names but the companies selling the "picks and shovels" of the data-center build-out: servers, switchgear, cooling, optics, memory and power. Almost every durable move started with a quantified demand surprise (guidance far above consensus, a tripled backlog, a gigawatt-scale contract, a first profit) and a single ignition day: a big gap up on several times normal volume, usually closing at a new 52-week high.

Start date matters. Stocks that crashed in 2022 look ordinary over five years but were the biggest winners from the low: Carvana +8,466% (after −99%), AppLovin +2,879% (after −92%), Palantir +3,384%, Robinhood +1,482%. Small-cap trough-to-peak moves were bigger still: Rigetti ~150x, Sezzle ~139x, GeneDx ~135x, D-Wave ~108x.

Many started as mid caps (CLS, STRL, FIX, BE, LITE, CRDO, POWL) and only later became large caps, so a scan limited to today's index members misses them early. Celestica trades on the NYSE and is not in the S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100.

Why they shot up: guidance shocks tied to a capex cycle
  • NVIDIA guided ~$11B vs ~$7.2B expected (May 2023) → +24% next day.
  • Broadcom sized its AI market at $60–90B for FY2027 (Dec 2024) → +24%, passed $1T.
  • Powell reported backlog nearly tripled to a record $1.3B (Aug 2023), before the data-center story attached to it.
  • Credo guided $115–125M off a much smaller base → +48% in a day.
  • Constellation signed the 20-year Microsoft deal to restart Three Mile Island → +22%. Bloom signed up to 1 GW with AEP → +59%.
  • Turnarounds ignited on first profits or debt fixes: Carvana (positive EBITDA guide, ~50% of float short), Sezzle (Q1 2024 profit beat all of 2023), Root (revenue +173%).

What held vs. what round-tripped. All 16 theme stocks checked (quantum, small nuclear, space, eVTOL, speculative AI) are now 47–87% below their peaks; several had little or no revenue when they spiked. About 10 of 15 backlog-and-earnings compounders are within ~40% of their highs. Earnings alone weren't enough: Celsius, Duolingo, Cava and Hims still fell 55–72% when growth slowed. The durable recipe: revenue already accelerating, a multi-year backlog, and demand tied to a measurable spending cycle, confirmed again every quarter.

Skeptics were still around after ignition (Palantir kept Hold/Sell ratings), and S&P 500 inclusion acted as a later accelerant (APP +11.6%, HOOD +15.8% the next session).

  • Ignitions cluster. In NVIDIA's May 2023 report week, Broadcom and Vertiv also broke out on heavy volume. Waves: AI hardware (Feb–Aug 2023), power (2024), post-election (Nov–Dec 2024), memory (Apr–Sep 2025). When a leader posts a blowout, scan its suppliers next.
  • The first ignition often isn't the one that pays. Micron (Dec 2023) and Dell went nowhere for a year with ~60% drawdowns before their real leg. The big trends came from repeated earnings gap-ups: Powell, AppLovin, Credo and Sezzle each had three to six.
  • Small caps were more extreme: 20–75% days on 5–85x volume (Root 85x, GeneDx 35x, AST SpaceMobile 22x).
Reality check: base rates and hindsight
  • Most stocks lose. About 4% of US stocks account for all the market's net gain over T-bills since 1926 (Bessembinder). Roughly 0.7–2% of stocks go 10x in 10 years.
  • Traits barely predict it. Observable characteristics explain only ~2% of which stocks become decade-long winners.
  • The proven signals give modest edges: momentum (~5.5%/yr after publication), nearness to the 52-week high (George & Hwang), the high-volume return premium (Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin), earnings-day volume plus surprise. Classic post-earnings drift has largely vanished from liquid US stocks since ~2006.
  • Volume alone makes breakouts less reliable. In Bulkowski's ~8,000-pattern study, heavy-volume breakouts failed 14% vs 5% for quiet ones and pulled back to the breakout 57% vs 18% of the time.
  • Failures look identical at ignition. Super Micro's Jan 2024 day (+36%, 7.3x volume, new high) was the perfect signature; it then fell 85% after a short report and its auditor resigning.
  • This study is hindsight too: the 23 ignitions were picked because they worked. How many lookalike days led nowhere was not measured.
How to catch the next ones: scan rules

1Wide universeInclude NYSE / NYSE American names outside the big indexes; price ≥ $5, 50-day dollar volume ≥ ~$5–20M
2Up-day qualityUp day closing in the top 40% of its range: (C−L)/(H−L) ≥ 0.6. Treat down-day volume spikes as distribution, not signals
3Ignition gapOpen ≥ 8% above prior close on ≥ 3x 50-day average volume, holding (close ≥ open or upper half of range)
4Trend gateC > SMA50 > SMA150 > SMA200, SMA200 rising over 21 sessions, within 25% of the 52-week high and ≥ 25% above the low; bonus for a new 252-day high
5Relative strength0.4·r63 + 0.2·each earlier quarter, percentile ≥ 80; bonus when the stock/SPY line hits a 52-week high before price
6Repeat & groupBoost names with ≥ 2 qualifying gaps in 12 months, and names whose industry peers flagged within 5 sessions
7Pocket pivotUp-day volume above the largest down-day volume of the prior 10 sessions: quieter accumulation before the gap
8Calendar hygieneDiscount opex, triple witching / S&P rebalance Fridays, Russell reconstitution and index-add days (mechanical volume)

Turnaround lane. Palantir, AppLovin, Vistra and Carvana ignited 20–72% below their highs, so the trend gate rejects them. Track them separately: ≥ 15% gap on ≥ 5x volume with strong 3-month relative strength. Expect more volatility.

Rank on a composite (setup flags, RS percentile, nearness to the high, then volume ratio), not on raw volume ratio. Then backtest the flags over 2015–2026 to get their real hit and failure rates before sizing anything.

Playbook: many small bets, re-enter second legs

Nearly every winner bottomed near 2025-04-04 (Vertiv −61%, Credo −61%, AppLovin −57%, Celestica −54%). A fixed −20% stop would have shaken you out of almost all of them. Pick one style:

  • Swing: buy the ignition close or next day's opening range; stop at the gap-day low; take ⅓–½ off near +20%; trail the rest on the 10/20-day average; risk ~0.5–1% of equity per trade.
  • Compounder: admit only names with accelerating revenue, growing backlog or rising margins; start small enough to survive a 50–60% drawdown; add on the 2nd and 3rd earnings gaps; exit on a broken thesis (guidance cut, accounting/auditor event, lost customer), not price alone.
  1. Hold many small, roughly equal positions; winners are rare and skewed.
  2. Re-enter names that re-ignite; Micron and Dell paid on the second ignition.
  3. Cut new-entry size after big market drawdowns; momentum crashes in panic rebounds.
  4. Exit a theme when several peers post high-volume down days together (quantum and nuclear peaked together Oct 7–15, 2025).
  5. Short interest and options flow: tie-breakers only.
